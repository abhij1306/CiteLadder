# Content formats

Name the chosen format in `save_document`'s format_id and follow its section. Choose the format from the buyer task; not every business needs a blog. No format has a minimum word count, a compulsory FAQ or a formulaic conclusion. Editorial notes stay out of the public copy.

## content_page — Website page

A service, feature, landing or other general page when nothing more specific fits. One H1, an opening that states the value at once, H2 sections for the reader's distinct questions (what it is and who it is for, outcomes with proof, how it works, fit and limits, next step), and the real call to action. Keep verified prices, geography and functional labels such as "Log in"; a homepage refresh is not a redesign. Deliver the copy section by section with metadata and link destinations.

## product_page — Product page

One purchasable offering. The exact product name as H1, a short value statement, key purchase facts, benefits tied to real attributes, a specifications table when it helps, purchase information (sizing, delivery, returns) when supplied, and a clear next step. Names, variants, prices and ratings stay exact.

## category_page — Category page

A group of products, services or subcategories. H1 and a short definition, who it is for, the main options and how to choose among them, then navigation onward. Keep the site's own taxonomy; keep it short when the category is simple.

## about_us — About page

Who the organization is, what it offers, who it serves and the facts that make it credible. A direct definition first, then offering, approach, people, locations and milestones as supplied. Concrete facts over mission language.

## article — Article or guide

For a reader who wants to learn, decide or complete a task. A specific H1, the useful answer first, H2 sections for the real follow-up questions, concrete examples or steps, and a closing takeaway that adds something. A tutorial includes enough detail to finish the task. Deliver title, description, URL, body, source links and suggested internal links.

## blog — Blog post

Lighter than a guide but still useful: the main takeaway in the first 50 words, one practical point per H2 with an example, and a short takeaway. Conversational and precise; no invented anecdotes.

## faq — FAQ page

Discrete questions phrased as real users ask them, each answered in its first sentence. As many as the evidence justifies, no near-duplicates, and no promise of rich-result gains.

## glossary_term — Definition

For "What is X?": a one- or two-sentence definition first, then how it works, why it matters, an example and commonly confused terms. Short.

## listicle — Roundup

For "best", "top" or "options" content. Scope and selection criteria first, one section per item with why it belongs and its trade-off, and how-to-choose guidance. Rank only when evidence supports a ranking.

## case_study — Case study

Only with real first-party customer or project information: snapshot, starting situation, approach, outcome with exact supplied metrics and dates, and lessons. Without metrics, write it qualitatively.

## comparison — Comparison or alternatives

For readers choosing between options. H1 naming the comparison, a short decision summary, explicit criteria, a table of supported facts, "choose X if…" guidance and trade-offs. Fair to competitors; no misleading checkmark tables.

## content_refresh — Content refresh

Read the existing page first. Fix changed facts, missing answers, weak proof and broken links; keep what works and the URL. Return exact changes or the full revised version, as asked, with a change log. Never update a date to simulate freshness.

## youtube — YouTube script

Spoken copy for one buyer question: a two- or three-sentence hook, what the video covers, logical sections, proof, limits and a next step, with on-screen notes in brackets. Deliver title, script, chapters marked approximate, a thumbnail concept and a description.

## tiktok — TikTok, Reels or Shorts

A very short hook, three to five quick beats of one idea each with on-screen cues in brackets, and a closing line.

## linkedin — LinkedIn post

One idea in the voice of a named person or the company: open with the idea before the "see more" cut, two to four short paragraphs with specifics, and a usable takeaway. Plain text; no engagement bait.

## x — X post or thread

A thread's first post carries the strongest claim, each next one a self-contained point, the last a takeaway; a single post is the strongest self-contained version. Within the platform limit.

## instagram — Instagram caption

A short hook, a scannable body that complements the visual, a relevant action and a few relevant hashtags. No clickable URL in the caption.

## reddit — Reddit or forum answer

Answer the question as supplied before mentioning the business, disclose the affiliation naturally, and link only when useful and allowed. Note that the live thread and community rules need checking before posting.

## newsletter — Newsletter

A subject line and preview text, an opening that states the payoff, two to four short sections and one primary next step.

## original_research — Original research or linkable asset

A research brief before results exist: question, population, method, data rights, analysis plan and limits. With real data, document methods and provenance and keep association apart from causation. Route distribution to Earned authority.
